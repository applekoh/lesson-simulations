Quick prediction (step 4)
Why does the cup fog at the dew point, not when it is merely colder than the room?

Air always contains some water vapour, but at any given temperature there is a maximum amount of vapour it can hold. That maximum gets smaller as the air gets colder. The dew point is the temperature at which this maximum equals the amount of vapour the air actually contains.

The thin layer of air touching the cup is cooled to the cup's surface temperature. If the surface is above the dew point, the maximum at that temperature is still larger than the vapour actually present, so the vapour stays a gas and the cup stays dry. If the surface is below the dew point, the maximum has dropped below the vapour present, so the extra vapour turns into liquid droplets on the cup. That is the fog.

So the test is the cup's surface temperature compared with the dew point, not compared with the room temperature.
