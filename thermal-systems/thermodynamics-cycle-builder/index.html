About this simulator

  • Steady flow; kinetic and potential energy changes are neglected; no pressure drops in heat exchangers, mixers or pipes.
  • Properties are read from the property tables by straight-line interpolation, the same way you would by hand: water from Tables A-4, A-5 and A-6, R-134a from Tables A-11, A-12 and A-13, and air as an ideal gas from Table A-17 (isentropic processes use the relative pressure, Pr2/Pr1 = P2/P1). Click any pipe to see the table rows used for that state.
  • Compressed liquid is treated as saturated liquid at the same temperature. Pump work is wpump,in = v1(P2 − P1)/ηP.
  • Gas cycles use the air-standard assumptions: combustion is heat added to air, and the exhaust is heat rejected to the surroundings. When a fuel is chosen, the fuel burned is (heat added ÷ combustion efficiency) ÷ heating value, with heating values from Table A-27. CO₂ assumes complete combustion.
  • Sign convention: ΔE = Q + W, with Q = Qin − Qout (positive for net heat input) and W = Win − Wout (positive for net work done on the fluid). For each device at steady state, 0 = Q̇ + Ẇ + Σṁinhin − Σṁouthout.
  • Entropy generation: Ṡgen = Σṁoutsout − Σṁinsin − ΣQ̇/T, where T is the temperature of the source, sink or surface the heat crosses. Isentropic devices can show ±0.0003 kJ/kg·K from table rounding; these are shown as zero.
  • The tables limit the range: water up to 1300 °C and 60 MPa, R-134a from −40 to 100 °C (superheated table up to 1.6 MPa), air from 200 to 2250 K.
